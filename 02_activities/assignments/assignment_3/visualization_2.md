### What software did you use to create your data visualization?
Microsoft Excel, using PivotTables to aggregate incident counts and a horizontal bar chart to display neighbourhood-level comparisons.

### Who is your intended audience?
The intended audience includes policymakers, municipal analysts, community organizations, and members of the general public who are interested in understanding how reported intimate partner and family violence is distributed across neighbourhoods.

### What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization?
This visualization highlights how reported incidents of intimate partner and family violence are unevenly distributed across neighbourhoods. By ranking the top ten neighbourhoods over the 2014–2025 period, the chart emphasizes concentration rather than implying causation or individual-level behavior.

### What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots?
A horizontal bar chart was selected to accommodate long neighbourhood names and to make comparisons intuitive. Bars were sorted in descending order to emphasize ranking, and a single neutral color was used to avoid unnecessary emphasis. Gridlines were kept subtle, and redundant elements such as legends were removed.

### How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization?
Data aggregation was performed using clearly defined PivotTable operations that can be repeated by any user with access to the dataset. While Excel relies on some manual steps, the transformation logic is transparent and documented, ensuring functional reproducibility even if visual styling varies slightly.

### How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?
Accessibility was addressed through clear axis labels, descriptive titles including the year range, and a layout that does not rely on color differences to convey meaning. The horizontal orientation improves label readability.

### Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?
Communities represented in the chart may be affected by how their neighbourhoods are interpreted in public discourse. Policymakers and service providers may also be impacted, as such visualizations can influence resource allocation and policy decisions related to violence prevention and support services.

### How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization?
Only neighbourhoods and total incident counts were included to maintain focus on spatial distribution. Temporal detail, relationship categories, and premises types were excluded to prevent overcomplicating the visualization.

### What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?
Consistent with the concept of data visualization as the “tip of the iceberg,” this chart depends on extensive unseen labour. This includes the work of individuals reporting incidents, frontline workers and community organizations supporting victims, administrative staff recording and maintaining data, and public-sector employees who manage data systems. Additional invisible labour includes caregiving and emotional work that enables institutional data collection and analysis to occur, even though it is not visible in the final visualization.
